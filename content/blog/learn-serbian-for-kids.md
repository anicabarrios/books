Want to **learn Serbian for kids**, whether it is a family language you want to pass on, a trip you are planning or just a fun new skill? Start with animals. Animal words are short, easy to picture and easy to love, which makes them a friendly first step into the language.

This guide gives you 12 Serbian animal words with pronunciation, the sounds Serbian animals make, and five simple ways to practice at home in just a few minutes a day. The Serbian words are written in the Latin alphabet, with the Cyrillic version next to them.

## Why animals make great first Serbian words

- **You can point to them.** Words that match a picture or a real animal are easier for young children to remember than abstract words.
- **Kids already love animals.** A child who is excited about the dog is much more likely to say "pas" again.
- **Many are short.** Words like pas, zec and slon are quick to say and quick to repeat.
- **They come with sounds.** Animal noises add a game: you can say "av-av" before you say "pas".
- **They are everywhere.** Pets, picture books, zoo trips and cartoons give you constant chances to practice.

### Serbian letters that look new

- **č** sounds like the "ch" in "church," and **ć** is a softer, lighter "ch"
- **š** sounds like "sh" and **ž** like the "s" in "measure"
- **đ** sounds like the "j" in "jam," only softer
- **nj** is one soft sound, like the "ny" in "canyon," and **lj** is like the "lli" in "million"

## Serbian animal sounds

Animal sounds are the quickest way to get a giggle. Here are six to try:

- **Dog:** av-av (ahv-ahv)
- **Cat:** mjau (m-yow)
- **Cow:** mu (moo)
- **Sheep:** be-e (beh-eh)
- **Duck:** kva-kva (kvah-kvah)
- **Bird:** ćiv-ćiv (cheev-cheev, with the soft "ć")

Try asking **Šta kaže pas?** (shtah KAH-zheh pahs), which means "What does the dog say?", and answer **Av-av!** Then swap in any animal from the table.

## Latin or Cyrillic? Serbian has both

Serbian is written in two alphabets, Latin and Cyrillic, and both are used every day. Serbian spelling is very close to pronunciation: you write it the way you say it, so once you know a letter, you know its sound.

Many families start with Latin letters because they look familiar to kids who read English, and add Cyrillic later. Showing both on a flashcard, like in the table above, helps children see that they are two ways to write the same word.

## 5 easy ways to practice Serbian with animals

1. **Learn one word a day.** Pick one animal, show a picture and say the Serbian word a few times in a row. Stick the picture on the fridge for the day.
2. **Color first, then name it.** While your child colors an animal, say its Serbian name three times: **Ovo je pas.** (This is a dog.) Repeating a word while the hands are busy is a relaxed way to learn. This is the idea behind [Learn Serbian with Animals](book:learn-serbian): color an animal, then meet its Serbian name. It is made for ages 3 to 7.
3. **Play the sound game.** Ask "Šta kaže ...?" and take turns being the animal.
4. **Add a color.** Try short phrases such as **Ptica je plava.** (The bird is blue.), **Mačka je crna.** (The cat is black.) and **Pas je braon.** (The dog is brown.) Color words change their endings to match the animal, so for now treat each phrase as one little chunk to copy. Words like mačka, ptica, riba, krava, ovca and patka all go with crvena (red), žuta (yellow), zelena (green) or bela (white): **Riba je žuta.** (The fish is yellow.)
5. **Bring in a Serbian voice.** If a grandparent, cousin or friend speaks Serbian, ask them to record themselves saying the words, or to say them on a video call. Children often copy the voices they love best.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the easiest way to teach a child Serbian at home?

Little and often. Five to ten minutes a day with pictures, animal sounds and coloring beats one long lesson a week. Use the words in real moments too: when you see a dog, say "pas".

### Should kids learn the Latin or Cyrillic alphabet first?

Either works. Many families begin with Latin letters and add Cyrillic later. Showing both side by side helps kids see that they are two ways to write the same word.

### How old should a child be to start learning Serbian?

Any age. Babies and toddlers can listen to the words and sounds long before they speak them, and kids ages 3 to 7 can start coloring and naming animals.

### Do I need to speak Serbian myself to teach it?

No, but it helps to listen along. Use pronunciation guides, ask a Serbian-speaking friend or relative to record the words, and learn together. Your child will enjoy teaching you a word too.
